# PRD summary: ADHD Budgeting App

## Problem

About 1 in 14 adults (roughly 366 million people) has ADHD. Budgeting apps assume steady attention, planning and daily habits, which ADHD makes hard. They fail in four ways:

1. **Too much setup:** long category lists drain motivation before any value shows up.
2. **Out of sight, out of mind:** balances hide behind taps.
3. **Daily habits required:** manual logging breaks the first time a user misses a few days.
4. **Shame after a slip:** red numbers and broken streaks push users away.

## Audiences (MVP)

- **Overwhelmed restarter:** has tried 2+ budgeting apps and quit each within weeks.
- **Surprise-hit spender:** keeps getting hit by forgotten bills, subscriptions and impulse buys.

## Value proposition

For adults with ADHD who keep falling off budgets, our app shows what is safe to spend in one glance and welcomes you back after any break, so staying on top of money takes minutes, not willpower.

## Key results (first 3 months after beta launch)

1. 40% of new users still active in week 4.
2. 60% of users who stop for 7+ days come back within 30 days.
3. Users report 30% fewer late fees or overdrafts in a month-3 survey.

## Competitors

Money apps (YNAB, Monarch, Rocket Money, PocketGuard, Copilot, EveryDollar) have the tools but not ADHD-first design. ADHD apps (Tiimo, Inflow) have the design but no money features. PocketGuard already shows a "left to spend" number, so our edge is recovery after a lapse, the purchase pause and calm design.

## Release plan

| Phase | Timing | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Weeks 1 to 4 | 6 of 8 testers finish setup in 5 minutes |
| MVP #1: closed beta | Months 2 to 4 | 40% of beta users still active in week 4 |
| MMP: public launch | Month 5 onward | Targets set after beta |

## Not in MVP #1

Investing, credit scores, detailed reports, streaks, financial advice, couples mode, coach sharing, savings goals.
